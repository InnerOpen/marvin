# Auto-reply rule examples

| People write | Keywords | Reply | Network |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asking what something costs | `price, cost, how much` | `Prices are on each product's page. If you tell me which one you mean, I'll point you to it.` | One network, or all |
| Asking where to buy | `where to buy, buy, stockist` | `You can order from our shop, and the stockist list is on the same page.` | All |
| Asking about shipping | `shipping, delivery, ship to` | `We ship worldwide; the shipping page lists costs and times by country.` | All |
| Asking for the recipe or instructions | `recipe, how did you make, instructions` | `The full steps are in this week's post on our site.` | One network |

Use the real page names and facts from the workspace, not these. Each reply names where the answer is instead of repeating it
(prices and times change; a link or a page name doesn't go stale).

## Words to avoid on their own

- `love`, `thanks`, `great`: too common; the rule answers comments that don't need an answer.
- A single letter or very short word: it matches inside names and other words.
- A brand's own name: every comment may include it.

## When not to write a rule

- The answer depends on who is asking (an order status, a refund). A reply to everyone can't be right, so leave it to a person.
- The comments are complaints. An automatic reply to an upset person reads badly; use a workflow that sends them to a person.
